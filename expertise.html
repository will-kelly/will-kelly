Expertise is not
the same as output.

The whole promise of AI enablement collapses the moment a team can't tell which parts of the work came from judgment and which parts came from a model. So I keep them separate on purpose. The AI accelerates production. My expertise governs the claims. The two never get to trade places.

Most AI failures I'm called in to fix aren't about a bad model. They're about a blurred line. Someone let the tool make a judgment call it was never qualified to make — a recommendation nobody could source, a "finding" with no expert behind it, a confident paragraph that fell apart the first time a real stakeholder pushed on it.

My work runs on a single discipline: the human owns the judgment, the model owns the labor. Every agent I build, every deliverable I ship, has an explicit seam between those two things. Below is how I actually draw it.

The division of labor

Who owns what

When these two columns stay separate, AI is a force multiplier. When they bleed into each other, it's a liability with good grammar.

HUMAN

Expertise owns

  • The diagnosis — what's actually broken, and why
  • Every claim and pattern that carries my name
  • The prioritization — which fix matters first
  • Judgment calls an org will have to defend in a budget meeting
  • What a client will actually tolerate versus what's theoretically correct
  • The final "ship it" — always
AI

Output owns

  • Structure — turning a mess of notes into a scored framework
  • Drafting — first passes I can react to instead of blank pages
  • Mechanics — formatting, consistency, tone-matching
  • Recall — surfacing context from prior calls and docs
  • Adversarial review — attacking my argument before a reader does
  • Speed — the reps, so I spend my hours on the judgment

The workflow

How the seam holds in practice

A repeatable loop, not a vibe. Expertise brackets the AI on both ends — it never runs unsupervised in the middle.

  1. 01 · Frame

    I set the problem and the standard

    Before the model touches anything, I define what "good" looks like and what claim I'd be willing to defend. The AI never gets to decide the question — only help answer the one I've framed.

  2. 02 · Produce

    The agent does the labor

    Structuring, drafting, scoring, recalling context — this is where AI earns its speed. I let it run wide and fast, because nothing here is final and nothing here is a judgment.

  3. 03 · Attack

    I turn a second agent against the first

    The Skeptical CIO stress-tests the output for weak claims and pattern-without-proof. AI is at its best as a critic of work — including work it helped produce. What survives gets my attention; what doesn't gets cut.

  4. 04 · Own

    I rewrite the judgment from experience

    The verdicts, the prioritization, the "here's what I'd actually do" — that comes from fifteen-plus years in enterprise tech, not from the model. If I can't source a sentence to my own expertise, it doesn't ship with my name on it.

The stance

Why the line is the whole point

AI doesn't make me an expert faster. It makes the expertise I already have reach further. Confuse those two and you ship confidence you can't back.

On what AI is for

The tell of AI-generated work isn't the prose — it's the absence of a person who'll stand behind it. I'm always that person.

On accountability

Enterprises don't fear AI because it's wrong. They fear it because no one can tell where the human judgment went. My job is to make that seam visible.
